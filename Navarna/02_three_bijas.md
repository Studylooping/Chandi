# 2. The three bījas ऐं · ह्रीं · क्लीं: what the texts say they stand for

This file reports **the texts' own cosmology**, with citations. It does not make claims about physics or the physical universe. Phrases like "primordial energy" or "governing the universe" are used only where a text or commentator says something equivalent, and the Sanskrit is quoted each time.

Labels: **[TEXT]** = primary text; **[COMMENTARY]** = traditional commentator; **[ACADEMIC]** = modern scholarship.

Sigla are as in file 01:
- **DSS7** = *Durgāsaptaśatī with seven commentaries*, <https://archive.org/details/saptashati-7-teeka-1>. Vol. 1 is `saptashati-7-teeka-1_text.pdf` and Vol. 3 is `saptashati-7-teeka-3_text.pdf`. Each DSS7 quote was read from the page image.
- **PR / VR** = Prādhānika- and Vaikṛtika-rahasya, sanskritdocuments.org `prAdhAnikarahasyam.itx` and `vaikRitikarahasyam.itx`. Their header says the source is the *Bṛhatstotraratnākara* (Nirṇayasāgar). Their sha256 matches the revision pinned in `chandi/fetch.py`.
- **AŚ** = Devī Atharvaśīrṣa, `deviiatharva.itx`.
- Verse numbers are those of the e-texts.

## 1. Short answer

- **No single primary text I read assigns the three bījas to the three guṇas outright.** The link is built in steps:
  1. **The Rahasyas.** The Prādhānika and Vaikṛtika Rahasyas describe a primordial **Mahālakṣmī who is *triguṇā*** (made of all three guṇas). From her come **Mahākālī (from tamas)** and **Mahāsarasvatī (from sattva)**, and from these three the gods, the cosmic egg and the world. **The Rahasyas do not mention the bījas.**
  2. **The Navārṇa ṛṣyādi** (quoted from the Ḍāmara tantra by the commentators; see file 01 §3) lists the three goddesses *Kālī, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī* as the devatās of the three-part mantra.
  3. **The Gita Press arrangement** places ऐं / ह्रीं / क्लीं at the heads of the three caritras, whose devatās are Mahākālī / Mahālakṣmī / Mahāsarasvatī.
  4. **Bhāskararāya (Guptavatī)** maps the bījas to the three aspects of Brahman, not to the guṇas: ऐं = *cit* = Mahāsarasvatī; ह्रीं = *sat* = Mahālakṣmī; क्लीं = *ānanda* = Mahākālī. He explicitly notes that some tantras reverse the first and third. He also reports an older view (*prāñcaḥ*) that Mahālakṣmī is to be contemplated with **rajas** predominant. That "rajas" appears in the commentary, not in the Rahasya verses.
  5. **The Kuñjikā-stotra**, a separate devotional text, gives yet another scheme: ऐं = creation, ह्रीं = preservation, क्लीं = desire (*kāma*).
- So "the three bījas govern the primordial energy" is **a commentarial and tantric synthesis**, and the tradition gives **more than one, partly inconsistent, mapping**. Details and quotations follow.

## 2. The primordial Śakti and the three guṇas: Prādhānika Rahasya (PR)

**[TEXT]**

**The question (PR 1).** The king asks for the *prakṛti* / *pradhāna* behind the avatāras:
> भगवन्नवतारा मे चण्डिकायास्त्वयोदिताः । एतेषां प्रकृतिं ब्रह्मन् प्रधानं वक्तुमर्हसि ॥ १ ॥

"Lord, you have told me the incarnations of Caṇḍikā. Brahman, please tell me their source (*prakṛti*), the primary one (*pradhāna*)."

**Mahālakṣmī, the first and triguṇā (PR 4).**
> सर्वस्याद्या महालक्ष्मीस्त्रिगुणा परमेश्वरी । लक्ष्यालक्ष्यस्वरूपा सा व्याप्य कृत्स्नं व्यवस्थिता ॥ ४ ॥

"The first of all is Mahālakṣmī, made of the three guṇas, the supreme ruler. With and without perceptible form, she stands pervading the whole."

**PR 6–7.** She fills the void with her own radiance, and then takes a second form made of tamas alone:
> शून्यं तदखिलं स्वेन पूरयामास तेजसा ॥ ६ ॥ शून्यं तदखिलं लोकं विलोक्य परमेश्वरी । बभार रूपमपरं तमसा केवलेन हि ॥ ७ ॥

This is the dark goddess named in PR 11: महामाया महाकाली महामारी क्षुधा तृषा । निद्रा तृष्णा चैकवीरा कालरात्रिर्दुरत्यया.

**PR 13–15.** From pure sattva she takes a moon-bright form, named in PR 15: महाविद्या महावाणी भारती वाक् सरस्वती …
> तामित्युक्त्वा महालक्ष्मीः स्वरूपमपरं नृप । सत्त्वाख्येनातिशुद्धेन गुणेनेन्दुप्रभं दधौ ॥ १३ ॥

**PR 16–23. Each of the three emits a male–female pair (*mithuna*).**
- **Mahālakṣmī** → a golden pair, Brahmā (Vidhi, Viriñci, Dhātṛ) and Śrī (Padmā, Kamalā, Lakṣmī) (PR 17–18).
- **Mahākālī** → "nīlakaṇṭha, red-armed, **white-limbed**, moon-crested" Rudra, and a white woman, Trayī-vidyā / Bhāṣā / Svarā (PR 20–21):
  > नीलकण्ठं रक्तबाहुं श्वेताङ्गं चन्द्रशेखरम् । जनयामास पुरुषं महाकाली सितां स्त्रियम् ॥ २० ॥
- **Sarasvatī** → Gaurī, and a **dark** man, Viṣṇu / Kṛṣṇa / Hṛṣīkeśa (PR 22–23).

**PR 25–28. The pairs are re-married, and the world arises from them.**
> ब्रह्मणे प्रददौ पत्नीं महालक्ष्मीर्नृप त्रयीम् । रुद्राय गौरीं वरदां वासुदेवाय च श्रियम् ॥ २५ ॥ स्वरया सह सम्भूय विरिञ्चोऽण्डमजीजनत् । बिभेद भगवान् रुद्रस्तद्गौर्या सह वीर्यवान् ॥ २६ ॥ अण्डमध्ये प्रधानादि कार्यजातमभून्नृप । महाभूतात्मकं सर्वं जगत्स्थावरजङ्गमम् ॥ २७ ॥ पुपोष पालयामास तल्लक्ष्म्या सह केशवः । महालक्ष्मीरेवमजा राजन् सर्वेश्वरेश्वरी ॥ २८ ॥

Translation:
- "Mahālakṣmī gave Trayī to Brahmā as wife, boon-giving Gaurī to Rudra, and Śrī to Vāsudeva.
- Uniting with Svarā, Viriñca produced the egg. The mighty Rudra, with Gaurī, split it.
- Inside the egg arose the whole set of effects beginning with *pradhāna*: the entire world, moving and unmoving, made of the great elements.
- Keśava, with Lakṣmī, nourished and protected it. Thus Mahālakṣmī, unborn, is the ruler of the rulers of all."

**PR 29.** She is both formless and with form, and bears many names:
> निराकारा च साकारा सैव नानाभिधानभृत् ॥ २९ ॥

**Colophon** (sanskritdocuments): "iti shrImArkaNDeyapurANe sAvarNike manvantare devImAhAtmye prAdhAnikarahasyaM sampUrNam || adhyAya 14".

**The bījas.** **PR does not mention ऐं, ह्रीं or क्लीं.** Its viniyoga (prefixed in the e-text) gives the deities and "navadurgAmahAlakShmIrbIjaM, shrIM shaktiH". The bījas enter only through the Navārṇa material (§4).

## 3. Vaikṛtika Rahasya (VR): guṇa labels for the three caritra goddesses

**[TEXT]** VR 1–2, 7, 14:
> त्रिगुणा तामसी देवी सात्त्विकी या त्वयोदिता । सा शर्वा चण्डिका दुर्गा भद्रा भगवतीर्यते ॥ १ ॥ योगनिद्रा हरेरुक्ता महाकाली तमोगुणा । मधुकैटभनाशार्थं यां तुष्टावाम्बुजासनः ॥ २ ॥
> सर्वदेवशरीरेभ्यो याऽऽविर्भूताऽमितप्रभा । त्रिगुणा सा महालक्ष्मीः साक्षान्महिषमर्दिनी ॥ ७ ॥
> गौरीदेहात्समुद्भूता या सत्त्वैकगुणाश्रया । साक्षात्सरस्वती प्रोक्ता शुम्भासुरनिबर्हिणी ॥ १४ ॥

So, in the Rahasyas' own words:

| Caritra | Episode | Goddess | Guṇa according to VR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Ch. 1) | Madhu–Kaiṭabha | Mahākālī = Viṣṇu's Yoganidrā | **तमोगुणा** |
| 2 (Ch. 2–4) | Mahiṣāsura | Mahālakṣmī = Mahiṣamardinī, born from all the gods' bodies | **त्रिगुणा** (not "rajas") |
| 3 (Ch. 5–13) | Śumbha–Niśumbha | Mahāsarasvatī, born from Gaurī's body | **सत्त्वैकगुणाश्रया** |

VR 24–25 then reaffirms their identity: "महालक्ष्मीर्महाकाली सैव प्रोक्ता सरस्वती".

## 4. Where the bījas are attached to these goddesses

### 4.1 The Navārṇa ṛṣyādi (Ḍāmara tantra, as quoted)

**[TEXT as quoted]** DSS7 Vol. 1, Guptavatī p. 12 (PDF 23); Nāgojībhaṭṭī p. 66 (PDF 47).
> … देव्यः प्रोक्ता महापूर्वाः काली लक्ष्मीः सरस्वती … स्याद्रक्तदन्तिकादुर्गाभ्रामर्यो बीजसन्ततिः । अग्निर्वायुः सूर्यस्तत्त्वं …

Two points about this verse:
- Read in order, the triads pair (Brahmā, Gāyatrī, **Kālī**, Nandā, Raktadantikā, Agni), then (Viṣṇu, Uṣṇik, **Lakṣmī**, Śākambharī, Durgā, Vāyu), then (Maheśvara, Anuṣṭubh, **Sarasvatī**, Bhīmā, Bhrāmarī, Sūrya). The Gita Press caritra viniyogas distribute them exactly this way (file 01 §3.2).
- The "bījas" named **in this verse** are Raktadantikā, Durgā and Bhrāmarī. These are the **names of future avatāras from DM Ch. 11** (see file 03), not the syllables ऐं ह्रीं क्लीं.

The same passage (Nāgojī p. 66) places **Mahālakṣmī** in the whole mantra:
> अस्मिन्नवाक्षरे मन्त्रे महालक्ष्मीर्व्यवस्थिता ।

### 4.2 The Gita Press text

**[TEXT]** `durga700.itx`:
- **ऐं** before 1.1, in the caritra of Mahākālī: "OM aiM mArkaNDeya uvAcha".
- **ह्रीं** before 2.1, Mahālakṣmī: "OM hrIM R^iShiruvAcha".
- **क्लीं** before 5.1, Mahāsarasvatī: "OM klIM R^iShiruvAcha". Also "klIM OM" after the last verse.

This positional pairing (ऐं–Mahākālī–tamas, ह्रीं–Mahālakṣmī–triguṇā, क्लीं–Mahāsarasvatī–sattva) is the one most readers infer. **However, it conflicts with Bhāskararāya's semantic mapping (§5.1).**

The edition also records other bīja schemes:
- Nāgojī (p. 66, PDF 47) gives one caritra scheme with **सौः / ह्रीं / क्लीं**, and notes "ऐंमिति बीजमाद्यचरितस्येत्यन्यत्र" (elsewhere ऐं is the first caritra's bīja).
- He gives another tradition for the whole Saptaśatī with **क्लीं** as its single bīja.

## 5. Bhāskararāya's interpretation (Guptavatī)

All quotes are DSS7 Vol. 1, pp. 10–13 (PDF 21–24), read from the page image. **[COMMENTARY]**

### 5.1 The bījas as cit, sat and ānanda

> सच्चिदानन्दात्मकपरब्रह्मधर्मत्वादेव शक्तेरपि त्रिरूपत्वम् । तत्र चिद्रूपा महासरस्वती वाग्बीजेन सम्बोध्यते । ज्ञानेनैवाज्ञाननाशान्निर्धूतनिखिलध्वान्तपदेन तद्विवरणं युक्तमेव । … एतेन सद्रूपात्मकमहालक्ष्मीरूपस्य हृल्लेखया सम्बोधनमिति व्याख्यातम् । … तेन आनन्दप्रधानेन महाकालीस्वरूपस्य कामबीजेन सम्बोधनमुक्तम् ।

| Bīja | Name in the commentary | Aspect of Brahman | Goddess addressed |
|---|---|---|---|
| ऐं | वाग्बीज / वाग्भव | **cit** (consciousness) | Mahāsarasvatī |
| ह्रीं | हृल्लेखा / माया / शक्तिबीज | **sat** (being) | Mahālakṣmī |
| क्लीं | कामबीज | **ānanda** (bliss) | Mahākālī |

His argument for ह्रीं → Lakṣmī, given that **श्रीं** is the well-known Lakṣmī-bīja:
> यद्यपि श्रीमित्येव बीजं महालक्ष्म्याः प्रसिद्धं न हृल्लेखा तथापि हकारशकारयोरूष्मत्वेन साजात्यान्नातीव भेदः । अत एव 'ह्रीश्च ते लक्ष्मीश्च पत्न्यौ' इति श्रुतौ । शाखान्तरे ह्रीपदस्थाने श्रीपदस्य पाठः ।

"Although Mahālakṣmī's well-known bīja is श्रीं, not the hṛllekhā, *ha* and *śa* are both sibilant/aspirate (*ūṣman*) sounds, so there is little difference. Hence the śruti 'Hrī and Lakṣmī are your two wives'. In another śākhā *śrī* is read in place of *hrī*." I did not look up the Vedic passage he cites (**UNVERIFIED**: its exact Saṃhitā location).

He acknowledges a reversed mapping in other tantras:
> एवं स कामबीज एव लकारस्य स्थाने रेफयोजनेन कालीबीजता … तन्त्रान्तरेषु कालीसरस्वत्योर्वास्तविकाभेदमभिप्रेत्य बीजयोर्वैपरीत्याद्व्यवहारोऽपि दृश्यत इति द्रष्टव्यम् ।

"…in other tantras, because Kālī and Sarasvatī are in reality non-different, the two bījas are also used the other way round." He also says: replace *la* with *ra* in the kāma-bīja and you get Kālī's bīja (क्रीं).

### 5.2 The colours in nyāsa, and the creation story

DSS7 Vol. 1, p. 13, PDF 24. He quotes a nyāsa rule (source not named at this point): the first bīja is to be meditated as "very dark" (कृष्णतरम्), the second as "sun-like" (सूर्यनिभम्), the third as "crystal-like" (स्फटिकाभासम्). He then explains why the first (Sarasvatī's) is dark and the third (Kālī's) is white:
> अत्र प्रथमतृतीययोर्बीजयोर्महासरस्वतीमहाकालीरूपयोः श्वेतकृष्णरूपत्वेऽपि वैपरीत्येन रूपनिर्देशो वास्तविकाभेदसत्त्वेन रहस्ये वक्ष्यमाणं सरस्वत्याः कृष्णाङ्गविष्णुजनकत्वं काल्याः श्वेताङ्गशिवजनकत्वं चाभिप्रेत्येति बोध्यम् ।

"Although the first and third bījas, being Mahāsarasvatī and Mahākālī, are white and dark, their colours are given reversed. This is because of their real non-difference, and with reference to what the Rahasya will say: that Sarasvatī begets dark-limbed Viṣṇu and Kālī begets white-limbed Śiva."

This matches PR 20 and PR 22 quoted above, so here the commentator ties the bījas directly to the Prādhānika creation narrative.

### 5.3 The guṇas: Mahālakṣmī as the "fourth", with rajas predominant (an older view)

DSS7 Vol. 1, p. 11, PDF 22:
> अत्र प्राञ्चः । तिसृणामपि देवीनां तुरीयया महालक्ष्म्या अविशेषेऽपि महालक्ष्म्या एव प्राधानिकरहस्ये 'सर्वस्याद्या महालक्ष्मीः' इत्यादिना तुर्यस्थाने गणनादितरयोर्देव्योस्तज्जन्यत्वस्य कथनात् । महालक्ष्मीजननस्य पार्थक्येनाकथनाच्चोपास्यदेवताया तुरीयत्वेऽपि तस्यां रजःप्राधान्येनेतरगुणयोर्न्यग्भावेनैव भावनमावश्यकमित्याहुः । तामेव च रीतिमाश्रित्य महालक्ष्मीबोधकहृल्लेखाया एव नवार्णे प्रधानबीजत्वादथर्वशीर्षे तावन्मात्रेण पुनः स्वातन्त्र्येणोद्धारस्यापि प्राधान्यध्वननार्थत्वात् ।

"Here the earlier [teachers] say the following.
- All three goddesses are non-different from the 'fourth' (*turīyā*) Mahālakṣmī.
- Yet in the Prādhānika Rahasya Mahālakṣmī alone is counted in the fourth place ('*sarvasyādyā mahālakṣmīḥ*'), the other two are said to be born from her, and her own birth is not narrated separately.
- Therefore, even though the deity worshipped is the 'fourth', she must be contemplated with **rajas predominant** and the other two guṇas subordinate.
- On the same principle, the hṛllekhā (ह्रीं), which denotes Mahālakṣmī, is the **principal bīja of the Navārṇa**. That is why the Atharvaśīrṣa extracts it a second time, on its own, to signal its primacy."

That separate extraction is AŚ 18 (वियदीकारसंयुक्तम्).

On PR 4 itself, Bhāskararāya (DSS7 Vol. 3, p. 280, PDF 91; image-verified) first sets out the hierarchy:
> ग्रन्थारम्भे वर्णितं धर्मिरूपं ब्रह्मैव चण्डिकापदवाच्यमुपास्यस्वरूपं तस्याश्च व्यष्टिरूपाणि त्रीणि महाकाली महालक्ष्मीर्महासरस्वती । तेन समष्टिरूपैव चण्डिका तुरीया धर्मिरूपा निर्गुणा किन्तु पञ्चमीति स्थितिः ।

"Brahman, the substrate (*dharmin*) described at the start of the work, is what the word Caṇḍikā denotes and what is worshipped. Its three individual forms are Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī. Caṇḍikā as their aggregate is the fourth. The *dharmin*, the guṇa-less, is in fact a fifth: that is the position."

Then, on *triguṇā*:
> त्रिगुणा सात्त्विकराजसतामसमूर्तित्रयसमष्टिरेव सर्वप्रपञ्चादिकारणम् ।

"'Triguṇā' means the aggregate of the three forms (sāttvika, rājasa, tāmasa), which is the cause of the whole manifest world." He reports that some, citing a Śiva-purāṇa verse, take "Mahālakṣmī" in PR 4 as a name of the "fourth", Caṇḍikā, not of one of the three. His evaluation that follows ("एतत् । न …") is partly illegible to me in the print, so I do not report its conclusion.

### 5.4 The universe as the object of the bījas: the "Cāmuṇḍā" gloss

DSS7 Vol. 1, p. 10 (PDF 21) and p. 11 (PDF 22). Bhāskararāya reads the mantra as ending in liberation from the world rather than in governing it. Cāmuṇḍā is the one who "devours the army (*camū*) made of space and the other elements", and *vicce* is the plea "release me". His summary of the meaning:
> काल्यै लक्ष्म्यै सरस्वत्यै चण्डिकायै नमोऽस्तु ते । अविद्यापाशहृदयग्रन्थिं विस्रस्य मुञ्च माम् ।

"Homage to you, Kālī, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī, Caṇḍikā: loosen the knot of the heart, the noose of ignorance, and set me free."

## 6. Two other primary sources

### 6.1 Devī Atharvaśīrṣa

**[TEXT]** AŚ 17:
> सैषा सत्त्वरजस्तमांसि । सैषा ब्रह्मविष्णुरुद्ररूपिणी ।

"She is sattva, rajas and tamas. She has the form of Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra."

AŚ 2 declares "मत्तः प्रकृतिपुरुषात्मकं जगत्" ("from me is the world, made of prakṛti and puruṣa"). AŚ 18 encodes ह्रीं as "the goddess's bīja that accomplishes every aim" (देव्या बीजं सर्वार्थसाधकम्). AŚ 20 encodes the Navārṇa (file 01 §2.1).

**The AŚ does not assign the three bījas to the three guṇas.** It identifies the Devī with all three guṇas and all three gods at once.

### 6.2 Siddha-kuñjikā-stotra

**[TEXT]** sanskritdocuments `siddhakunjikaa.itx`. Its colophon attributes it to "Rudrayāmala, Gaurī-tantra", with a variant attribution to the "Ḍāmara-tantra". That attribution is **UNVERIFIED**.
> ऐङ्कारी सृष्टिरूपायै ह्रीङ्कारी प्रतिपालिका । क्लीङ्कारी कामरूपिण्यै बीजरूपे नमोऽस्तु ते ॥ ८ ॥

"To you as ऐं, whose form is creation; as ह्रीं, the protector; as क्लीं, whose form is desire: homage to you in the form of the bījas."

This is a **third** mapping, by function: creation, preservation, desire. It matches neither the guṇa labels of the Rahasyas nor Bhāskararāya's cit/sat/ānanda exactly.

## 7a. Lakṣmī Tantra (Pāñcarātra): the same cosmogony, a different bīja system

**[TEXT]** Sources:
- Sanskrit: V. Krishnamacharya's Adyar edition (1959), OCR at <https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.326285/2015.326285.Lakshmi-Tantra_djvu.txt>.
- English: Sanjukta Gupta's translation (reprint Delhi 2003), OCR at <https://archive.org/download/laksmitantratranslationbysanjuktaguptareprintdelhi2003lakshmitantra/LAKSMI-TANTRA-translation-by-Sanjukta-Gupta-reprint-Delhi-2003-LAKSHMI-TANTRA_djvu.txt>.
- I checked the quoted lines in both OCR files. The Sanskrit OCR is noisy; obvious OCR errors are normalised in brackets.

**Mahālakṣmī as the first, with the other two as guṇa-forms (LT 9.7–8):**
> आदौ देवी महालक्ष्मीः स्मृताहं परमेश्वरी । अभूवं [?] पुनर्द्वेधा कृष्णा ब्राह्मीति रूपतः ॥ गुणत्रयविभागेन रूपमेतत्परं मम ।

Gupta: "In the beginning, I, the holy Goddess, (manifested myself) as the goddess called Mahālakṣmī. Next I became two by assuming the forms of Kṛṣṇā and Brāhmī. These are my three supreme forms, as classified by the three guṇas." Her note: "Kṛṣṇā is the dark goddess Mahākālī and Brāhmī is Mahāvidyā. Cf. ch. IV, 57–67."

This is the Prādhānika Rahasya's scheme in another text. According to the sub-agent, who read Gupta's translation, LT 4.57–67 and 5.1–15 also give the ten names of Mahākālī, the names of Mahāvidyā/Sarasvatī, and the three pairs re-married as Trayī–Brahmā, Gaurī–Rudra, Padmā–Vāsudeva. **I did not check ch. 4–5 myself.**

**The bījas in the Lakṣmī Tantra.**
- Gupta's translation of LT 26.14 (checked): "This bīja (viz. aim) is the source of creation and is known as vāgbhava."
- According to the sub-agent's reading of Gupta's ch. 26, not checked by me:
  - the kāma-bīja is क्लीं ("the great śakti of Pradyumna");
  - the trio forming the "Tripurā" vidyā is ऐं, क्लीं, सौः;
  - ह्रीं is **not** part of that trio.
- So the Lakṣmī Tantra shares the *cosmogony* of the three goddesses. As far as was found, it does **not** attach ऐं/ह्रीं/क्लीं to Mahālakṣmī/Mahākālī/Mahāsarasvatī. Its description of ऐं as "the source of creation" agrees with the Kuñjikā-stotra's "ऐङ्कारी सृष्टिरूपा".
