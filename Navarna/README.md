# Navarṇa research notes (Chandi project)

These are research notes on the Navārṇa mantra (ऐं ह्रीं क्लीं चामुण्डायै विच्चे), its three bījas, and the chronology of the Devī-Māhātmya episodes.

**Method**
- Every factual statement cites a source that was actually opened.
- Quotations from *Durgāsaptaśatī with seven commentaries* were read from the page images, not only from OCR.
- Each file keeps three things apart: **[TEXT]** (what the scriptures say), **[COMMENTARY]** (what traditional commentators interpret), and **[ACADEMIC]** (modern scholarship).
- Anything not checked is marked **UNVERIFIED**.

## Files

### [01_navarna_origin.md](01_navarna_origin.md): the mantra, its uddhāra, ṛṣyādi and sources

- **Uddhāra.** The coded extraction is Devī Atharvaśīrṣa v. 20 (वाङ्माया ब्रह्मसूस्तस्मात्…). Bhāskararāya (Guptavatī, DSS7 vol. 1 p. 10) decodes it letter by letter: वाक्=ऐं, माया=ह्रीं, ब्रह्मसूः=क्लीं, then चा-मुं-डा-यै, then विच्चे.
- **Why "nine".** The mantra is "nine" by vowel count, although it has 24 letters (Guptavatī).
- **The ṛṣyādi verse** "नवाक्षरस्य ऋषयो ब्रह्मविष्णुमहेश्वराः । छन्दांस्युक्तानि मुनिभिर्गायत्र्युष्णिगनुष्टुभः । देव्यः… काली लक्ष्मीः सरस्वती…" is quoted in the Guptavatī (p. 12) and by Nāgojībhaṭṭa (p. 66). Nāgojībhaṭṭa places it inside the Ḍāmara-kalpa procedure. Nāgojī rejects as "निर्मूल" (baseless) a *variant* caritra-wise scheme circulated under the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa's name, together with its per-chapter verse counts, because it conflicts with the Kātyāyanī Tantra's mantra division. That variant differs from the Gita Press viniyoga: it gives **Anuṣṭubh** (not Gāyatrī) for the first caritra and Anuṣṭubh-Jagatī for the third (page image checked, vol. 1 p. 68).
- **Sources.** The Ḍāmara, Kātyāyanī, Marīci, Vārāhī, Haragaurī, Lakṣmī and "Yāmala" tantras are known here only through these commentators' quotations. The Rudrayāmala attribution exists only in the colophon of the Siddha-kuñjikā-stotra, which gives Ḍāmara as a variant.
- **Aṅga–aṅgin.** Bhāskararāya rejects the view that the Saptaśatī is enclosed by the Navārṇa as its principal. The Navārṇa is the "topic" of the tantric sections and is aṅga only to the *beginning* of the recitation. The 13 chapters (MP 74–86 in his count) form one mālā-mantra, which the Ḍāmara calls "नवार्णज" ("born of the Navārṇa").
- **Origin.** No text narrates who "composed" the mantra. Its "seers" are Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśvara. **No historical date for the mantra was found.**

### [02_three_bijas.md](02_three_bijas.md): what ऐं, ह्रीं, क्लीं stand for

- **The Rahasyas.** The Prādhānika and Vaikṛtika Rahasyas give the cosmology: Mahālakṣmī, triguṇā and first of all, emits Mahākālī (tamas) and Mahāsarasvatī (sattva). Their three pairs produce the cosmic egg and the world. **They do not mention the bījas.**
- **The bīja–goddess link** comes from the Navārṇa ṛṣyādi and the Gita Press caritra placement.
- **Bhāskararāya's mapping:** ऐं=cit=Mahāsarasvatī, ह्रीं=sat=Mahālakṣmī (the principal bīja), क्लीं=ānanda=Mahākālī. He notes that other tantras reverse ऐं and क्लीं. He reports the older view that Mahālakṣmī is contemplated with rajas predominant.
- **Other mappings.** The Kuñjikā-stotra gives ऐं=creation, ह्रीं=protection, क्लीं=desire. The Lakṣmī Tantra shares the three-goddess cosmogony but uses a different bīja system.
- **Conclusion.** The sources give **several, partly inconsistent, mappings**. All of them are the texts' cosmology, not physical claims.

### [03_chronology.md](03_chronology.md): when did the episodes happen?

- **[TEXT]** Medhas tells the stories to Suratha in the **Svārociṣa (2nd) manvantara**. Suratha will be **Sāvarṇi, the 8th Manu**. Madhu–Kaiṭabha happens "at the end of a kalpa". Mahiṣa and Śumbha–Niśumbha are simply "purā". Ch. 11 prophesies a second Śumbha–Niśumbha in the **28th yuga of the Vaivasvata (7th) manvantara**, killed by the Nanda-born Vindhyavāsinī, followed by Raktadantikā, Śatākṣī/Śākambharī/Durgā, Bhīmā and Bhrāmarī (GP 11.41–55).
- **[COMMENTARY]**
  - The 28th yuga is the Dvāpara–Kali junction, Kṛṣṇa's time (Guptavatī, Nāgojī, Śāntanavī).
  - The Lakṣmī Tantra gives the 40th / 50th / 60th yugas for Śatākṣī / Bhīmā / Bhrāmarī. On that basis Bhāskararāya says all six later avatāras are still **future**.
  - The Lakṣmī Tantra also puts Mahiṣa in Svāyambhuva and Śumbha–Niśumbha in Tāmasa, which conflicts with the DM frame.
- **[ACADEMIC]**
  - The text is dated about **5th–6th c. CE** (Pargiter 1904; Coburn 1984 "fifth or sixth", 1991 "sixth"). Yokochi argues for the 8th c., known here via Policardi 2024.
  - A firm lower limit is the Nepalese manuscript of **998 CE** (Shāstrī 1905). The Jodhpur/Dadhimatī inscription is read as 608 CE (Bhandarkar) or 813 CE (Mirashi).
  - Buffalo-slaying goddess images go back to **Kuṣāṇa Mathura** (e.g. Govt. Museum Mathura 2317; Berlin I 5817). Gupta reliefs survive at **Udayagiri**. The earliest images have no attested name or myth.
- **No historical, archaeological or astronomical evidence dates the mythological events, and no citable astronomical dating attempt was found.**
